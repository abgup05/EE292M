Specimen: made-up data

Every component of Evidence Desk once, in the theme you chose: Paper, a printed research note, or Terminal, the same note as a dense dark screen. Example Corp is fictional, and so is every filing, figure, 8-K and outcome here. The version-13 components at the top draw records the backend does not send yet; each says the shape it expects (docs/design.md).

Marks and controls

Version 13: a compiled test on a claim

Shape: {claim_id, expression, outcome: holds|fails|cannot_compute, outcome_label, operands: [{name, label, display, period_label, form, accession, filed, filed_label, url}], arithmetic, margin_display, note}. The outcome, arithmetic and margin are the backend's strings.

Version 13: the thesis over time

Shape: {outcome_labels, claims: [{claim_id, text, points: [{filed, filed_label, form, accession, url, outcome}], flips: [{filed, to, label}]}]}. Each claim's test at each filing date: holds above, cannot compute between, fails below. The flips are ringed; the backend names them.

Version 13: as of a date

The control in the thesis form takes {min, max, value, hint?}; the banner on a run made as of a past date takes run.as_of.requested = {date, label, line}.

Version 13: the restatement ledger

Shape: {rows: [{metric_label, period_label, first: {display, form, filed_label, url}, later: {…}, change_display, note}]}. The first value is struck through; both filings are named.

Version 13: the peer strip

Shape: {industry_label, metrics: [{metric_label, unit_label, company: {ticker, display, value}, min, q1, median, q3, max: {value, display}, percentile_label, peers: [value]}]}. The dot is the company; the box is the middle half of its industry, the bar its median. The percentile is the record's sentence.

              The marks on this page
              ✓Supported
              Verified findings support the claim and none challenge it. On a finding, the same mark means it supports the claim.
              ✕Challenged
              Verified findings challenge the claim and none support it. On a finding: it challenges the claim.
              ±Mixed
              Verified findings point both ways.
              ?Not judged
              Passages were quoted but no engine judged them.
              ∅Not addressed
              No verified quote or figure bears on the claim.
              •Context
              A finding that informs the claim without taking a side.
              ▲ Limit
              Something limits this record: a partial or failed run, a coverage limit, an error, a source not read, a failed check.
              ✎ Model-written
              Written by a language model and not checked for meaning by a person. A second model's reading has its own marks, next.
              ◎Agrees
              A second model, not shown the first one's reasoning, read the finding's evidence and agrees that it supports the statement and its stance. Still a model's reading, not a person's: its reason is one click away.
              ◐In part
              The second model agrees only in part; its reason is shown under a double violet rule. A result of the check, not a limit of the record: read the quote and decide.
              ▲ Period not found
              A year a verified finding names that is not in its quotes, its cited figures or the period of a filing it quotes. It is reported only: it withholds nothing and changes no verdict. A year one below the filing's own is often the comparison year a report prints beside its own; read the quote to see which year it means.
              ▲ Predates an 8-K
              Every filing a finding cites is older than an 8-K whose item bears on its claim's subject. A claim whose verdict rests on such a finding carries a caution. Reported only: no verdict changes.
              ! Refused
              The server refused your input; the message says how to fix it.
              dashed border
              Not final. A dashed border marks one of three things: a claim no engine judged (Not judged); a decision you have still to make (Undecided, not reviewed yet); a run still in progress, with its claims not yet decided.
              ƒFixed rule +Added −Removed ~Reworded #Numbers only
              Under each claim of a finished run: paragraphs that changed between the newest annual and the newest quarterly report the run read and the filing of the same form filed before each (fetched for the comparison; the run may not have read it), and that share words with the claim or the search queries the run wrote for it. Listed by a fixed rule (ƒ: computed by a fixed rule; not checked by a person); no model chose or judged them, they are not findings, and none counts toward the verdict. In a reworded pair, struck through was dropped and underlined was added.
              ◇ Complete
              The run's status, at the top of a result: an open diamond when complete, three dots while running, the limit mark when partial or failed. A status is never a filled tag.
              Time line
              One axis of dates from the record: tall bars are the annual and quarterly reports the run read (a dot above one means a quote comes from it); filled diamonds are 8-Ks with an item of a substantive category, small circles routine ones; the hatched band is the time between the newest filing read and the run. Hover over or focus a mark to read it and open the filing.
              Sample record
              A grey tag is a label about the page itself.
              highlighted
              A quote found word for word in the filing, shown in its surrounding text.
              underlined accent text
              Something you can click.
              ▣Accepted
              Your decision: you accepted this finding, so it counts toward the verdict after your review. A dark filled tag is always your own decision: nothing the engine, a model or a rule produced is drawn that way.
              ☒Rejected
              Your decision: you rejected this finding, so the verdict after your review leaves it out.
              ☐Undecided
              Your decision is still to make; dashed, because the verdict after your review is provisional until you decide.
