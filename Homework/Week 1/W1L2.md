# W1L2: Something Claude Got Wrong

I was doing some equity research on an oil producer and asked Claude for a quick rundown of WTI crude prices in 2008 so I could compare them against the company's results. It told me oil averaged about $125 a barrel that year, peaked at $147 in July, and ended the year around $96.

The $147 peak was right, which is probably why I almost took the rest at face value. But the year-end number bugged me. Oil crashed during the financial crisis in late 2008, so ending the year near $100 didn't make sense. I checked the EIA data: the 2008 average was closer to $100, and oil actually finished the year around $45, less than half of what Claude said. Its $96 was basically where oil *started* 2008, so it looks like it mixed up the beginning and end of the year.

For someone who was looking at it for research that would lead to potential positions with real money this was a big problem. Plugging a $125 average into a model overstates revenue by about 25%, and the year-end price is what companies used to value their reserves at the time, which is why a lot of producers took huge write-downs in Q4 2008. With Claude's numbers, the biggest thing that happened to oil that year (the crash) just disappears.

When I asked Claude why it got this wrong, it said it doesn't look up prices from a database. It generates answers from patterns in its training data. The $147 peak shows up in almost every article about oil in 2008, so it recalled that one reliably, but it doesn't have the same strong memory of the full-year average or the exact closing price. It said the ~$96 figure was probably the end-of-2007 price, which shows up in the same articles, and it attached that number to the wrong end of the year. It also admitted it should have said it wasn't sure instead of giving me numbers that sounded exact.

Reasonable as it may be it is not acceptable for high risk environments like trading. What bothered me most was how confident the original answer sounded. My takeaway is to use Claude for context, but check any number that goes into a model against the actual source, especially when it doesn't match what you remember about that period.
