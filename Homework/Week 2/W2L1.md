#### Assumptions

1. Each row is one order and `order_id` identifies it. An ID that appears twice with the same content after cleaning is a duplicate entry, not a second sale.
2. The true value of an order is units × unit price × (1 − discount). The inputs back this up: each product carries one list price in every row (Dock $250, Monitor $450, Tablet $700, Air Laptop $1,200, Pro Laptop $1,800), and discounts only take the values 0, 5, 10 and 15%. The `revenue` column is the less reliable field.
3. Revenue counts only Completed and Shipped orders. A shipped order counts as a sale: the goods have left and it is normally invoiced at that point.
4. Pending orders are not revenue yet. Several have sat pending since January, so they are not near-certain sales either.
5. Cancelled orders are not revenue.
6. Returned orders count as zero, whatever the refund column says. ORD-1061 is returned with a $0 refund; I read that as a refund not yet recorded, not as a kept sale.
7. A refund on a completed or shipped order is a partial refund or credit and comes off revenue. Refunds on rows already excluded (ORD-1008, cancelled, $100; ORD-1022, pending, $2.4K) have no effect.
8. "Total revenue" means net revenue after refunds. Gross is shown next to it.
9. "Average order value" means net revenue per counted order. Each order holds a single product line, so orders and rows are the same unit.
10. "Conversion rate" has to be a proxy. The only funnel in the file is order placed → kept sale, so conversion = kept sales ÷ unique orders placed, with pending orders counted as not converted as of this snapshot.
11. Regions are read as recorded after merging spellings. The file uses 4 states (CA, IL, NY, TX) next to 4 compass regions and gives no mapping between them, which fits a setup where large states are carved out as their own territories.
12. "Performed best" means highest net revenue.
13. ORD-9001 to ORD-9004 are real orders and stay in. They use list prices, valid discounts and in-range dates, but they are flagged below.
14. Amounts are in US dollars and the period is January 2 to March 30, 2026. Customer-name spellings (Acme, ACME INC., Acme Incorporated and so on) don't affect these four answers, so I left them as written.

### How I got there

1. **Read the whole file.** 120 rows, 13 columns, all dated in Q1 2026. Almost every field has format noise: `$` signs, thousands commas, padded spaces, `1.2K` shorthand, discounts written as `10%` or `0.1`, mixed-case labels and four date formats.
2. **Cleaned each field** into numbers and standard labels. Ten status spellings collapse to five statuses (Completed, Shipped, Pending, Cancelled, Returned), and 27 region spellings collapse to eight labels.
3. **Removed duplicates.** Six IDs appear twice: ORD-1008, -1019, -1034, -1052, -1073 and -1092. In each pair every field matches after cleaning; only case, date format or region spelling differ. Keeping one of each leaves 114 orders. I also checked for the same customer, date, product and units under two different IDs and found none.
4. **Checked the ID sequence.** ORD-1001 to ORD-1110 is complete with no gaps (110 orders). The four extras, ORD-9001 to -9004, are all Acme, all sold by Jordan and all from the web source, which is why they get a flag.

The column holds 94 exact values, 14 "K"-rounded values and 6 blanks (ORD-1005, -1029, -1040, -1080, -1091, -1096). Of the 94 exact values, 88 match to the cent and 3 differ only by whole-dollar rounding (ORD-1041: 1,188 vs 1,187.50; ORD-1050 and ORD-1064: $212 vs $212.50). Four values are wrong:
