    How a run works

    1. Enter a ticker, the decision you face, and the claims your thesis rests on.
    2. The backend reads the company's latest annual report (10-K), the quarterly reports (10-Q) filed after it, and the figures the company reported in machine-readable form (XBRL). It finds the passages that bear on each claim.
    3. An engine reads those passages and proposes findings, each citing quotes or reported figures. Every quote is checked word for word against the stored filing, and every number in a finding is matched against its quotes and figures. A finding that fails a check is withheld.
    4. Each claim's verdict follows by a fixed rule from the findings that passed. Whether a quote supports or challenges the claim is the engine's reading of it, not something the checks prove: open the claim and read the quote in place before you rely on it.

        How to read this page
        ✓Supported
        Verified findings support the claim and none challenge it. On a finding, the same mark means it supports the claim.
        ✕Challenged
        Verified findings challenge the claim and none support it. On a finding: it challenges the claim.
        ±Mixed
        Verified findings point both ways.
        ?Not judged
        Passages were quoted but no engine judged them. A dashed border always means not judged or provisional.
        ∅Not addressed
        No verified quote or figure bears on the claim.
        •Context
        A finding that informs the claim without taking a side.
        ▲ Limit
        Something limits this record: a partial or failed run, a coverage limit, an error, a source not read, a failed check.
        ✎ Model-written
        Written by a language model and not checked for meaning.
        ! Rejected
        Your input was rejected; the message says how to fix it.

                  Terms used on this page
                  10-K
                  The annual report a US public company files with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC).
                  10-Q
                  The quarterly report, filed for each of the other three quarters.
                  XBRL
                  The machine-readable tags in a filing that state each reported number, its period and its unit. Reported figures on this page are read from them.
                  Item
                  A numbered section of a filing, such as Item 1A, Risk Factors, or Item 7, Management's Discussion and Analysis (MD&A).
                  Period end
                  The last day of the quarter or fiscal year the filing reports on. Fiscal years need not match calendar years.
                  Filed
                  The date the company submitted the filing to the SEC. "Retrieved" is when this run downloaded it, in UTC.
                  Accession number
                  The SEC's identifier for one filing; hover over a form to see it.
                  Derived, restated
                  A derived value was worked out from reported ones (for example a fourth quarter as the year less nine months). A restated value was reported differently by a later filing.
                  CIK
                  The SEC's numeric identifier for a company.
                  EDGAR
                  The SEC's public filing system, where the filings linked on this page are published.
