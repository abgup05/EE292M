## 2. New: what changed that bears on each claim (milestone 8, `capstone-v8`)

The tool had two halves that did not meet: a verdict per claim, and a comparison of two filings. Now, for each claim of a run, it lists the paragraphs that changed in the filings that run read, when they share words with the claim. Each filing is compared with the filing of its form before it, found by the run's own accession number. Every changed paragraph is ranked against the claim with the same keyword scoring the run's retrieval uses; a paragraph needs two words in common, the five best are listed, and one printed under two Items is listed once.

No model is involved and nothing in the list is a verdict. The page and the brief say so beside it.

On the recorded example (five claims about NVIDIA, 802 changed paragraphs in two comparisons), the first paragraph listed under "Gross margin is expanding" is one the newest annual report added: "Gross margin decreased in fiscal year 2026 as our business model transitioned from offering Hopper HGX systems to Blackwell full-scale datacenter solutions." Under the claim about suppliers the first is about vendor questionnaires under the cybersecurity Item. Both are in `Capstone/evidence_desk/docs/claim-changes.md`, the second as the feature's limit.

## 3. New: does the second model reject what does not fit? (review 5, F6)

The meaning check had read 77 real findings and rejected 1. Nobody knew whether it would reject a finding whose quotes do not support it. The evaluation keeps each verified finding of the golden set that has a quote, with its claim, statement and stance, and gives it real quotes that belong to another finding.

| Pairs | Count | Judged not supported | Partly | Supported |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Another company's quotes | 76 | 75 | 1 | 0 |
| Another claim's quotes, same company | 76 | 74 | 2 | 0 |
| The findings' own quotes | 76 | 1 | 10 | 65 |

What this is worth: it is the first evidence that the check discriminates at all. What it is not worth: a swapped quote is usually about another topic, which is the easy case; the answers are a stand-in's (Claude Haiku in six fresh contexts, given the prompts under neutral file names and not told they were planted); and each request held only mismatched items. A quote on the right topic that does not carry the statement is the hard case, and it is not measured.

## 4. New: segments, geographies and customers from the filing's own tags (milestone 9, `capstone-v9`)

The figures this tool has shown since version 3 come from the SEC's company-facts data, which carries no revenue by segment, product or geography and no customer shares. Those are what the example thesis is about, and they are in each filing's own inline XBRL. Version 9 reads them: every tagged number with its concept, period, unit, scale and dimension members, built into five breakdowns, each value with its change from the year before and each breakdown with a check that its members add up to the filing's own tagged total.

From NVIDIA's annual report for the year ended 25 January 2026, as tagged:

| Revenue, USD millions | Fiscal 2026 | Change | Fiscal 2025 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Data Center | 193,737 | +68.2% | 115,186 |
| China including Hong Kong | 19,677 | -21.4% | 25,048 |
| United States | 149,617 | +93.1% | 77,482 |
| Total | 215,938 | +65.5% | 130,497 |

One direct customer is tagged at 22% of revenue and another at 14%. So three of the example's five claims now have a figure the tool could not show before.

**Checked against the filing's own words.** The tests find the same quantities in the text the project's other parser extracts: the table row "Data Center $193,737 $115,186", the sentence "Data Center revenue for fiscal year 2026 was up 68% from a year ago", and "sales to one direct customer represented 22% of total revenue". That proves the reader puts each number under the right member, period and scale. It does not prove the company's number: the printed figure and the tagged one are the same characters.

**Run on eight companies**, annual and quarterly reports, sixteen filings: wherever a filing tags a total, every breakdown's members add up to it. Getting there took real filings breaking the first version five times: members that are sums of other members (NVIDIA, Tesla), two splits of one total on the same axis (Microsoft, Amazon), segments reconciled through eliminations (Coca-Cola, JPMorgan), a customer share qualified by geography that had leaked into share of revenue, and Costco's 16-week quarter and 36-week year to date, which fell outside the period lengths the code expected and were dropped without a word. Each fix began as a failing test built from an excerpt of the filing.

**What it does not do.** The labels are made from tag names ("Wearables Homeand Accessories"). No person has compared a table with the printed one. And a finding cannot cite these figures yet: offering them to the engine would change its prompt and invalidate every recorded answer, so for now they sit beside a run and decide nothing.
