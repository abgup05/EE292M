## 2. New: what changed that bears on each claim (milestone 8, `capstone-v8`)

The tool had two halves that did not meet: a verdict per claim, and a comparison of two filings. Now, for each claim of a run, it lists the paragraphs that changed between each filing the run read and the filing of its form before it, when they share words with the claim or with the search queries the run wrote for it. The pair is found by the run's own accession number. Every changed paragraph is ranked with the same keyword scoring the run's retrieval uses; a paragraph needs two words in common, the five best are listed, and one printed under two Items is listed once.

No model is called to make the list and nothing in it is a verdict. The page and the brief say so beside it.

On the recorded example (five claims about NVIDIA, 802 changed paragraphs in two comparisons), the first paragraph listed under "Gross margin is expanding" is one the newest annual report added: "Gross margin decreased in fiscal year 2026 as our business model transitioned from offering Hopper HGX systems to Blackwell full-scale datacenter solutions." Under the claim about suppliers the first is about vendor questionnaires under the cybersecurity Item. Both are in `Capstone/evidence_desk/docs/claim-changes.md`, the second as the feature's limit.

*Two sentences of this section were wrong when it was first handed in, and the third referee found both (section 5): it said a paragraph shares words "with the claim", and that the paragraphs changed "in the filings that run read". The rule counts the run's search queries too, and the earlier filing of each pair is fetched for the comparison and never read by the run.*

## 3. New: does the second model reject what does not fit? (review 5, F6)

The meaning check had read the golden set's real findings and rejected 1. Nobody knew whether it would reject a finding whose quotes do not support it. The evaluation keeps each verified finding of the golden set that has a quote, with its claim, statement and stance, and gives it real quotes that belong to another finding.

| Pairs | Count | Judged not supported | Partly | Supported |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Another company's quotes | 76 | 74 | 2 | 0 |
| Another claim's quotes, same company | 76 | 76 | 0 | 0 |
| The findings' own quotes | 76 | 1 | 10 | 65 |

What this is worth: it is the first evidence that the check discriminates at all. What it is not worth: a swapped quote is usually about another topic, which is the easy case; the answers are a stand-in's (Claude Haiku, given the prompts under neutral file names and not told they were planted); and each request held only mismatched items. A quote on the right topic that does not carry the statement is the hard case, and it is not measured.

*The first two rows first read 75, 1, 0 and 74, 2, 0. Sixteen of the 48 requests were answered again later the same day, after a fix changed the Costco findings they were built from (section 7).*

## 4. New: segments, geographies and customers from the filing's own tags (milestone 9, `capstone-v9`)

The figures this tool has shown since version 3 come from the SEC's company-facts data, which carries no revenue by segment, product or geography and no customer shares. Those are what the example thesis is about, and they are in each filing's own inline XBRL. Version 9 reads them: every tagged number with its concept, period, unit, scale and dimension members, built into five breakdowns, each value with its change from the year before and each breakdown with a check that its members add up to the filing's own tagged total.

From NVIDIA's annual report for the year ended 25 January 2026, as tagged:

| Revenue, USD millions: the breakdown, and the row as the tool labels it | Fiscal 2026 | Change | Fiscal 2025 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| By product or service: Data Center | 193,737 | +68.2% | 115,186 |
| By geography: China Including Hong Kong | 19,677 | -21.4% | 25,048 |
| By geography: US | 149,617 | +93.1% | 77,482 |
| Total (tagged, no dimension) | 215,938 | +65.5% | 130,497 |

One direct customer is tagged at 22% of revenue and another at 14%. So three of the example's five claims now have a figure the tool could not show before.

**Checked against the filing's own words.** The tests find the same quantities in the text the project's other parser extracts: the table row "Data Center $193,737 $115,186", the sentence "Data Center revenue for fiscal year 2026 was up 68% from a year ago", and "sales to one direct customer represented 22% of total revenue". That proves the reader puts each number under the right member, period and scale. It does not prove the company's number: the printed figure and the tagged one are the same characters.

**Run on eight companies**, annual and quarterly reports, sixteen filings: wherever a filing tags a total, every breakdown's members add up to it. Getting there took real filings breaking the first version five times: members that are sums of other members (NVIDIA, Tesla), two splits of one total on the same axis (Microsoft, Amazon), segments reconciled through eliminations (Coca-Cola, JPMorgan), a customer share qualified by geography that had leaked into share of revenue, and Costco's 16-week quarter and 36-week year to date, which fell outside the period lengths the code expected and were dropped without a word. Each fix began as a failing test built from an excerpt of the filing.

**What it does not do.** The labels are made from tag names ("Wearables Homeand Accessories"). No person has compared a table with the printed one. "Adds up" is the result of a search over the arrangements real filings use, not of one sum. At version 9 a finding could not cite these figures; version 10 is that step (section 6).

## 6. New: a finding can cite the tagged figures (milestone 10, `capstone-v10`)

A run can now ask to be offered the figures of section 4: each member's value and its change from a year earlier, for the newest annual and quarterly report it read, at most 40, the largest members of every breakdown first. They go to the engine after the other figures and into the record with them, so the citation gate, the number check, the page and the brief treat a citation of one like any other. It is off by default, because the figures change the engine's prompt and a recorded answer belongs to the exact prompt it answered.

To see what it changes, the example was recorded twice on one day by the same stand-in models, each given one request under a neutral name and told only to answer it: once without the option and once with it. Both use the plan already recorded for the identical prompt, so each claim is shown the same passages.

| | First recording (stand-in reminded of two rules) | Recorded again, no reminder | No reminder, tagged figures offered |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Findings verified, of those proposed | 18 of 18 | 18 of 18 | 18 of 19 |
| Numbers checked; not found | 25; 0 | 22; 0 | 35; 1 |
| Figures offered; citations in verified findings | 37; 9 | 37; 8 | 37 and 40 tagged; 20 |
| Verified findings with no excerpt, figures only | 1 | 1 | 3 |
| Verdicts on the five claims | supported, supported, mixed, mixed, mixed | supported, supported, supported, mixed, mixed | supported, supported, supported, mixed, supported |

- **The referee's ninth finding, measured.** Without the reminder every quote still verified and no number went unmatched.
- **What the option changed is what the findings cite**: Data Center revenue for the fiscal year under the growth claim; customers at 22% and 14% of revenue and at 25%, 18% and 13% of receivables under the concentration claim; revenue under the member "China Including Hong Kong", down 21.4%, under the claim about export controls, where the recording without the option had cited only the whole company's revenue growth.
- **What it does not show is a changed verdict.** The second and third columns differ on claim 5, under which no finding cites a tagged figure, and the first and second differ on claim 3 with identical prompts. One answer per prompt cannot tell the option's effect from asking again.
- **The recording found a fault in this project's own check.** Its first replay withheld a true finding because "at fiscal year-end 2026" was read as the quantity 2026. Fixed with a test. The finding that is still withheld states a true sentence it did not quote; the rule holds a statement to what the finding itself cites.

## 8. New: is the year a finding names in its evidence? (milestone 11, `capstone-v11`)

The referee's finding 2a was accepted as a limit in version 10 and built in version 11: a check that reads the years a verified finding's statement names and looks for each in the finding's own quotes, its cited figures and the period of each filing it quotes. It tells a fiscal label ("fiscal 2026") from a date ("July 26, 2026") from a bare year, and it reads each company's fiscal calendar from its newest annual report, because NVIDIA's quarter ended in July 2026 is in its fiscal 2027.

It reports and withholds nothing. That was decided before it was built: a check that withholds changes recorded verdicts, and its false alarms had not been measured.

**Measured on real findings.** The verified findings of the golden set name 34 years; all 34 are in their evidence. The three recordings of the example name 10, 12 and 12; all found. No false alarm, on findings none of which is known to be wrong.

**Measured on the same findings made wrong.** For each of the 25 golden-set findings that name a year, every year in the statement was moved by the same amount and the evidence left alone. No model is involved.

| Years moved by | Findings | Flagged | Not flagged |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| one year back | 25 | 21 | 4 |
| two years back | 25 | 24 | 1 |
| three years back | 25 | 25 | 0 |
| one year forward | 25 | 23 | 2 |

Five of the seven that passed did so because the quote itself prints the planted year: a sentence that compares a quarter with the year before contains both years. That is the wrong column of a table, the commonest real slip, and a check that asks only whether the year appears cannot see it. The plan's next milestone, table rows that keep their column headings, is what it waits on.

**Planting found a fault that the unit tests had not.** The first run flagged 19 of the 25 moved one year forward. Four of the six misses were dates: "as of July 26, 2027" passed against the report for the quarter ended 26 July 2026, because 2027 is that filing's fiscal-year label. A date names a calendar year. Fixed from a failing test; the count is 23.

The check and its measurement are in `Capstone/evidence_desk/docs/period-check.md`.

**What this reading showed.** The review found its two real faults in the parts that looked finished: a restore check that had passed at every version could not have failed on the case it exists for, and the one statement about where a live run's time goes had never been measured live.
